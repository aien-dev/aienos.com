The Crumb Doctrine

No receipt, no crumb: the epistemic constitution of the machine.

Drake Stapleton · October 2, 2026
v1.45 pagesNot peer reviewed
Read PDF LaTeX source DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23547808 Cite

Abstract

The Crumb Doctrine is the epistemic constitution of the Aien architecture. Its rule is simple: no receipt, no crumb. A crumb is a fragment of verified knowledge admitted only with a receipt recording the verifier, the specification, the dependency closure, the environment, and the exact result.

Claims are admitted through verification, not prose. Semantics defines behavior; specifications identify claims; receipts record evidence for those claims under explicit assumptions; policy determines admissibility; authorization remains separate. The doctrine defines the check, build, verify, and seal lifecycle and requires fail-closed admission: anything unverified is reported as not run, never as passed.

Doctrine paper; not peer reviewed.

What the doctrine does not decide

The doctrine defines how a claim is admitted, not whether the specification is the right one. A valid receipt can record a result for a badly chosen specification. The receipt proves that the check ran under the stated assumptions; it does not prove that the assumptions are sufficient.

An unverified claim is reported as not run, never as passed. The lifecycle is fail-closed: check, build, verify, seal. No receipt, no crumb.
