\section*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Preface}

I am writing these papers because machine intelligence needs a foundation it can stand on, and I want that foundation to be public.

The stack these papers describe is simple to state. Conversation produces proposals. Semantic objects make proposals explicit. Verification produces evidence. Crumbs make verified knowledge reusable. The Crumbline preserves its history. The Turing measures whether a new explanation earns its complexity on unseen evidence. T/J measures how physically expensive that gain was.

On measurement, I keep two quantities distinct. T\_ideal is the complexity-adjusted held-out log-loss improvement: what the mathematics says the gain is. T\_real is the complexity-adjusted improvement in an actual reference-coded bitstream: what an encoder really produced. On September 29, 2026, TY-1+TY-2 recorded the first profile-bound idealized Turing result: +2,559,679.825 T\_ideal. H1, the claim that T\_ideal approximates T\_real within the declared bound, remains open until the reference coder closes the bridge. I state the result's standing plainly: TY-1+TY-2 is an internally replayed descriptive result under historical profile V0. The experiment record reports separation of fitting, selection, and evaluation seeds, but independent blinding was not established, and the original corpus is not publicly downloadable. It does not constitute full qualification under the current protocol. The full evidence ledger is in the formal paper.

The Crumb Doctrine is at v1.4: the epistemic constitution. Omega proceeds next to Omega Core Semantics v0.1. The eighth paper, the Atemporal AIEN Postulate v1.1, proposes that an AIEN computation is defined by causal dependence rather than by time; it is a separate postulate, and the seven papers above stand unchanged.

\subsection*{The doctrine is becoming executable}

On October 2, 2026, the Crumb Doctrine gained its first executable spine across four repositories. aien-architecture merged ADR 0029 (``Verified Crumbs''), making Omega's verified library the dependency-resolution authority. aien-protocols merged VerifiedCrumbV1: the protocol spec with 17 golden vectors, malformed and refusal vectors, a C checker, and a failure taxonomy (MISSING\_RECEIPT, RECEIPT\_HASH\_MISMATCH, STALE\_RECEIPT, UNVERIFIED\_DEPENDENCY, UNKNOWN\_VERIFIER\_PROFILE, VERIFIER\_TOO\_OLD). omega hardened omega\_library\_insert to refuse missing or all-zero receipt hashes, require dependencies to already exist, and restrict BOOTSTRAP admission to genesis alone, with no flag able to unlock it. aien-sovereign-core merged aien-closure: a verify-closure binary plus CI that fails closed on unverified dependencies, missing receipts, hash mismatches, unpinned dependencies, cycles, stale receipts, undeclared imports, tainted artifacts, and unknown verifier profiles.

The pipeline is now: protocol, then Omega admission, then dependency closure verification, then CI enforcement.

I state the boundary honestly. This establishes verified artifacts, receipts, and closure checks. It does not yet establish the full Omega language semantics, the five identifiers as one universally enforced runtime model, the complete sealed foreign-crumb ABI, or the full check/build/verify/seal toolchain. Those remain proposed or partial. aien-test Slice C (digest cache) is proposed as PR \#170, not yet merged.

I name the identifiers as the Omega language defines them: \texttt{SEMANTIC\_ID}, \texttt{SPEC\_ID}, \texttt{CLAIM\_ID}, \texttt{RECEIPT\_ID}, and \texttt{SEAL\_ID}. An earlier draft of this preface said ``four''; the language defines five, and I correct the number openly rather than silently. Enforcing all five as one runtime model is itself proposed, not done.

The verdict I draw: receipt-and-closure checking is implemented and exercised in CI; repository-wide enforcement is not yet active. The doctrine is becoming executable, but it is not yet a property the machine enforces.
