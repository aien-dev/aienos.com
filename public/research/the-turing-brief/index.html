The Turing: A Unit of Verified Explanatory Gain

The three-page plain-language brief: what the Turing measures, what it does not, and what would prove it wrong.

M. Drake Stapleton · October 2, 2026
v1.43 pagesNot peer reviewed
Read PDF LaTeX source Cite

Abstract

The Turing (T) is a unit of verified explanatory gain: one Turing is one bit of net held-out description-length improvement against a declared baseline, after paying for the explanation itself, under a frozen measurement profile.

This brief states what the unit measures, what it explicitly does not measure, and what would prove the interpretation wrong, in plain language. The full definitions, proofs, and falsification criteria live in the formal paper.
